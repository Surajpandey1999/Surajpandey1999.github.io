01 — THE AI REVOLUTION

Artificial Intelligence is no longer the future.

AI has moved from research laboratories into everyday products, workplaces, public institutions and strategic competition. Its rapid adoption creates enormous opportunities—but also difficult questions about jobs, privacy, power and accountability.

02 — OPPORTUNITIES

What can AI change?

⚡ Productivity

Automation and AI assistants can accelerate routine knowledge work, coding, analysis and decision support.

🏥 Healthcare

AI can support diagnosis, medical research, drug discovery and access to health services.

🌾 Agriculture

Forecasting, crop monitoring, precision agriculture and advisory tools can improve farm decisions.

🏛️ Governance

AI can improve citizen services, document processing, policy analysis and administrative efficiency.

04 — INDIA

Where does India stand?

India is moving beyond simply consuming AI products toward building an ecosystem around compute, datasets, models, applications, skills and startups. The IndiaAI Mission is a major pillar of this effort, while India's large digital public infrastructure and technology workforce provide important advantages.

INDIA'S AI MOMENT

Compute + Data + Talent + Digital Public Infrastructure + Startups + Responsible AI

05 — POLITICAL SCIENCE

AI is also a question of power.

For political science, AI can be studied through elections and political communication, public opinion, policy analysis, digital democracy, surveillance, algorithmic governance, misinformation, regulation and the changing relationship between citizens and the state.

06 — GEOPOLITICS

AI is becoming a source of national power.

AI competition connects technology with economic security, semiconductors, data, cloud and compute infrastructure, military capabilities and diplomatic influence. The strategic competition among major powers is therefore not only about better algorithms—it is also about who controls the infrastructure and supply chains behind them.

07 — THE BIG QUESTION

Will AI remain a technological revolution—or become a new source of economic, political and geopolitical power?

The answer will depend not only on technical capability, but on institutions, regulation, public trust, access to infrastructure and the choices societies make about how AI should be governed.
