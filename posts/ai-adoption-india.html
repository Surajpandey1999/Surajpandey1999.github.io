Rapid Artificial Intelligence Adoption: Pros, Cons and Where Does India Stand?

Understanding AI's uses, India's emerging AI ecosystem, political implications, governance challenges and the geopolitics of a technology becoming a source of national power.

By Suraj Pandey · 03 September 2026 · Research & Analysis

AI is no longer just a technology story.

It is increasingly a story about economies, public institutions, information, labour, political power and national strategy.

AI AdoptionIndiaPoliticsGeopolitics

Artificial Intelligence is moving from an emerging technology into everyday economic, administrative and strategic systems.

Why rapid AI adoption matters

AI has moved from research laboratories into workplaces, consumer products, public institutions and strategic competition. Its rapid adoption creates enormous opportunities—but also difficult questions about jobs, privacy, power and accountability.

Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports that organizational AI adoption reached 88% of surveyed organizations in 2025, while generative AI reached 53% adoption in three years. citeturn0search0

Major uses of AI

AI includes machine learning, computer vision, natural-language systems, generative models, speech systems and increasingly agentic systems.

  1. Education: tutoring, translation, feedback and personalized learning.
  2. Healthcare: imaging support, research, clinical decision support and drug discovery.
  3. Business: customer support, forecasting, coding, document processing and knowledge management.
  4. Government: multilingual services, document workflows, citizen support and data analysis.
  5. Agriculture: crop monitoring, disease detection and resource optimization.
  6. Security: cyber defence, intelligence analysis, logistics and simulation.
  7. Politics: communication, content generation, sentiment analysis and information discovery.

The pros: opportunities

Productivity

AI can accelerate repetitive cognitive tasks and free time for judgment and creativity.

Access

Students, researchers and small businesses can access capabilities previously limited by cost or expertise.

Science

AI is increasingly supporting discovery across biology, chemistry, physics and other fields. citeturn0search7

Where does India stand?

India is not currently the leading producer of frontier AI models or private AI capital, but it has major advantages: a large digital population, a deep technology workforce, strong digital public infrastructure, a huge domestic market and an increasingly explicit AI strategy.

Stanford's 2026 public-opinion data reports that more than 80% of employees in India and several emerging economies reported regular or semi-regular workplace AI use in 2025. India also saw a sharp rise in AI nervousness, showing that adoption and concern can rise together. citeturn0search2

India's challenge: move from being a major user of AI to becoming a meaningful builder, researcher, infrastructure provider and rule-shaper.

India's AI initiatives

AI in Political Science

AI is not merely an information-retrieval tool for Political Science; it is becoming an object of political analysis. Key areas include elections and political communication, public opinion, policy analysis, digital democracy, surveillance, algorithmic governance, misinformation, regulation and the relationship between citizens and the state.

Power and the state

Technological capacity can expand administrative, intelligence and economic power. AI therefore raises classic questions about authority, accountability and state capacity.

Democracy

AI can widen access to information while also enabling targeted persuasion and synthetic media. The key issue is who controls these systems and under what rules.

AI and geopolitics

AI competition connects technology with economic security, semiconductors, data centres, electricity, cloud infrastructure, talent, military capabilities, standards and supply-chain resilience.

Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports that the U.S.-China frontier-model performance gap has effectively closed on its tracked comparison, while the U.S. remains strong in top-tier model production and private investment; China is exceptionally strong in research output, citations, patents and industrial deployment. citeturn0search1

This makes AI sovereignty important: maintaining meaningful national agency over domestic AI capabilities and critical infrastructure. citeturn0search12

What comes next?

  • AI literacy becomes a basic professional and civic skill.
  • Human oversight remains essential for high-impact decisions.
  • Compute and energy become strategic infrastructure.
  • AI governance connects domestic regulation with international diplomacy.
  • Education emphasizes verification, reasoning and responsible AI use.

Conclusion

Artificial Intelligence is becoming a general-purpose technology with consequences far beyond the technology sector.

For India, the strategic question is bigger than adoption. India must build domestic capability while remaining open to global innovation, encourage research while protecting citizens, and participate in shaping international AI rules.

For Political Science, AI opens new research into power, governance, political communication, state capacity, political economy and international relations. For geopolitics, it adds a new layer to competition: computational and technological capability.
